# Vinayagar collection

Edit `tools/ganesha_src.json` or `tools/ganesha_tamil_src.json`, then run `node tools/ganesha-build.mjs` and
`node tools/check-ganesha.mjs`. The app reads the generated static
`src/content/ganesha.json`; it performs no transliteration at runtime.

The collection includes the requested seven Sanskrit titles, both the 16-name
and 108-name hymns requested by the user, and the Tamil Vinayagar Agaval:

| Sankata Nashana | Pranamya shirasa devam | 8 |
| Shodasha Nama | Sumukhashchaikadantashcha | 3 |
| Runa Vimochana | Smarami devadevesham | 8 + closing verse |

| Santana Ganapati | Namostu gananathaya | 6 |

The Tamil additions correct the supplied attributions: Paalum Thelithenum is
the Nalvazhi invocation, not a section of Agaval. Aindhu Karathanai is labelled
as an invocation accompanying Thirumanthiram; the Tamil Virtual Academy's
commentary distinguishes it from the body of the work. Kariya Siddhi Malai is
described with the traditional Kachiyappa attribution, not Arunagirinathar's.
The chosen text begins Pantham Agatrum, not the supplied Mannulakil verse.
Kaithala Niraikani retains its full ending about Valli's marriage. Its mountain
reference is explained as Meru, not Palani.

The supplied four-line modern-sounding “Vinayagar Kavacham Tamil summary” is
not added as a traditional text: its source and authorship could not be
confirmed. The historical kavacham begins Valarsigaiyaip Paraparamai, as seen
in Project Madurai's pmuni0907 edition. Do not silently substitute the supplied
prayer for that work or imply that an edited summary is its original.

Ancient/traditional source texts only are transcribed. Modern translations,
commentaries, audio recordings and website assets are not reproduced. Source
links accompany each entry. Tamil meanings are new plain-language paraphrases
for Divine Life, not scholarly word-by-word translations. They remain editorial
drafts requiring a knowledgeable Tamil/Sanskrit reader's review before claiming
an authoritative edition. Agaval's yoga terminology admits multiple traditional
interpretations, noted in the relevant meanings.

Variant choices are identified in the entry descriptions. Sankata Nashana's
Narada Purana attribution is described as traditional, rather than asserting an
unverified chapter. Siddhi Vinayaka has a different 20-verse namesake; the app
uses the eight-verse Vighna Nivaraka hymn. Runa Vimochana has a different
Srishtyadau namesake; the app uses the nine-verse Smarami version.

Daily reading instructions are simple optional guidance. The texts' own time
references (tri-sandhya, sandhya, morning) are distinguished from general
Chaturthi practice. The Siddhivinayak Temple's published worship schedule
supports the latter: https://siddhivinayak.org/temple-schedule/ . No weekday,
direction, count or promised fertility/financial outcome is invented as a
mandatory rule. Phalashruti claims are translated as devotional claims.

Tamil is Agaval's original language. The reader falls back to Tamil when the
Sanskrit script preference is selected; the Latin option is transliteration.
Sanskrit Tamil readings use superscript aspiration/voicing markers (e.g. க²,
க³, க⁴). These are pronunciation aids, not a substitute for teacher-led oral
learning. Existing device speech remains synthetic reading, not a new recorded
chant or a verified guide to Sanskrit pronunciation.
